JJ’s Ice Cream Kiosk

DURATION
14 weeks
SKILLS
UI/UX, Motion Graphics
TOOLS
Figma, Krita, Illustrator, Adobe After Effects

Concept

An easy, efficient, and stress-free way to order a refreshing ice cream, built for multitasking parents and the kids who want to order for themselves.

Overview

JJ’s Ice Cream is an outdoors “mom and pop” ice cream shop in Upton, Massachusetts, serving over 75 different flavors of ice cream, with options for frozen yogurt, sherbet, sorbet, and zero sugar as well.

Project Goal

My goal is to curate an easy, efficient, and stress-free option for anyone looking to get a refreshing ice cream; especially those multitasking parents.

For children looking to input their own orders, the kiosk should provide language, illustrations, and directions that are clear, fun, and easy to understand.

Hardware Constraints

Screen

Austin Freestanding kiosk; a 22" all-in-one multi-touch screen, with a 1920×1080 resolution in landscape orientation. It’s serviced from the front, easy to fix quickly for routine maintenance.

Accessibility

Multilingual interfaces (English, Spanish, and Portuguese options) height-accessible installations, and voice- activated functions.

Placement

Kiosk would be located in an outdoor setting, close by to the ordering windows to maximize space usage and minimize lines. This would also make it easier for the customer to get their ice cream after ordering.

Content

60 total ice cream flavors, plus froyo, zero sugar, sorbet, and sherbet. Three sizes (1, 2, or 3 scoops), 23 toppings, and 8 cones (with the option of a cup instead), specialty sundaes, and drinks.

Workflow

The welcome screen covers account sign-in, rewards, and allergy selection.

A user may sign into their loyalty account to access favorited menu items, frequent orders, and points to be used for discounts on their next purchase, encouraging customers to return.

Help and accessibility options are available as a global menu at all times, ensuring the customer does not get stuck, and may ask for help wherever needed.

Language options are available globally as well, and may be switched at any time throughout the process.

Physical Mockups

Physical mockups were made to-scale in order to determine what sizes, placements, grids and buttons would work best during actual use. This helped rule out issues such as buttons being too small, hard to read text, and other user frustrations.

This was helpful to visualize the true scale of the screen and how to map out elements in a way that makes it easy and intuitive for real world users to navigate.

Styleguide

Verona Bold is used for the welcome screen header, Kit Sans is used for everything else. Primary Blue is used for side nav, item title bar, and main call-to-action buttons.

The illustrative style is intended to provide a fun and playful feel to the kiosk. They are mainly targeted towards families and kids, and provide a family-friendly and welcoming experience.
